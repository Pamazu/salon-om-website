## The feeling comes first

From the first moment, the words invite each visitor to feel something within. By the end of the page they feel calm and curious, and booking becomes their own choice. Write toward three feelings:

- **Calm and ease:** a moment to breathe.
- **Wonder and curiosity:** the gentle pull toward a next step.
- **Permission:** you deserve a peaceful moment for yourself.

## Maggie is the source

**One Source. Two Practices.** Maggie Woodward is at the center. She has two practices, and they are equal:

- **Salon OM**, an Aveda Concept Salon, for **guests**.
- **Inner Work with Maggie**, coaching, for **clients**.

People find Maggie three ways: an Aveda search, word of mouth for Maggie the stylist, and word of mouth for Maggie the coach. The first screen welcomes all three, with two equal doors. Neither practice is "also" or "the other one". Each one opens onto the other as an equal.

## How we write

**Our rhythm**

- **Warmth first, then a clear truth.** Open with care, then say the plain thing. ("Tea in your hands, shoulders dropping. Before anything begins, we talk.")
- **Triplets and short landing lines.** Lists of three ("your hair, your life, and how you would like to feel"), then a short line that lands ("This time is yours.").
- **Sacred words are capitalized:** Joy, Love, GRATEFUL, Self. Write them exactly as Maggie does: "GRATEFUL" in full capitals, the others with a capital first letter.
- **Always spoken in the positive.** Say what is there, not what is missing: "room to be fully heard", not "no judgement"; "real care takes its time", not "real care can't be squeezed".

**Our care**

- **Calm punctuation:** periods, never exclamation points.
- **Gentle words:** may, could, often.
- **What people often experience, rather than promises.** "Guests often leave lighter in spirit", not "You will leave transformed".
- **A California review of every word before launch.** Nothing ships until that review is done.

**Voice**

- Speak to one person, as "you". Short sentences, one idea each. Plain words over clever ones.
- Salon OM speaks as "we" and names Maggie in the third person. Inner Work is softer and more inward, and quotes Maggie's own words.
- No hype ("life-changing", "amazing", "cult following"), no urgency ("hurry", "limited", "last chance"), no emoji.

## The journey down the page

Each world follows Maggie's six steps, and each step carries one of J's conversion stages:

| Maggie's step | Conversion stage | What the words do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 The hook | Hook | A first feeling that invites them in: a gentle question, a motivation, a hint of what OM means |
| 02 Understanding | Establish authority: name their problem | Name what they carry, gently and without assumption |
| 03 Authority | Establish authority: experience | 28 years in the chair, the training and the origin story; the team |
| 04 Story and care | Storytelling: objections | Meet each quiet hesitation with warmth and patience (will they listen, will I be rushed, will it last) |
| 05 The offer | Features and benefits | Salon rituals and the Inner Work path, clearly laid out; why Maggie, why Aveda; the door to the other practice |
| 06 Begin | Summarize, then CTA | Voices of our guests, a line that recaps the visit, then one calm next step |

The copy deck that maps every slide to these stages is the StoryMind record "Salon OM 2.2 copy deck: CRO pass, both worlds" (19 Oct 2026).

## Rules for this option

- **The elements stay in the films and the structure, not in the words.** The five films still run, but the copy speaks of what the visit gives you. The one exception is the OM line, which names the elements by design: it is the brand's own sentence.
- **Two equal doors.** Wherever one practice points to the other, use the pairing line ("One Source. Two Practices.") and an equal button ("Enter Salon OM →", "Enter Inner Work →").
- **Aveda facts come only from the list in `shared.md`.** Nothing else about Aveda without a source.
- **The team:** Maggie Woodward (Founder), Katie Paonessa (Stylist), Chesney (Salon Concierge). Never a placeholder photo of a real person who isn't them: show their initial until their portrait arrives. Maggie's portraits stay labelled "Placeholder portrait" until real photos arrive.
- **Testimonials:** only Maggie's chosen guest quotes, word for word, initials only (L.T., C.M., G.A., S.). Never invent or edit a quote. No Inner Work testimonial until real words arrive.

## Words on film

- Text on a film is read in a few seconds while the picture moves: one idea, short lines (about 40 characters), never more than three lines of body.
- The big word carries the slide. The body explains it, the specs give the detail. Never repeat the big word in the body.
- On clay slides the line can run longer (about 56 characters), and the copy can breathe a little more.

## Mechanics

- **Sentence case** for headlines, buttons and labels ("Book your visit"). Eyebrows and labels are typed in sentence case too; the design sets them in capitals. Locked lines keep their own capitals ("Where Beauty and Inner Work Belong", "One Source. Two Practices.").
- **American spelling:** color, center, program. (Maggie's guide calls for a California review.)
- **Serial comma** in our own copy ("honesty, compassion, and courage"), as in Maggie's own writing. Quotes from Maggie and from guests stay exactly as written.
- **Brand names:** "Salon OM" (never "Salon Om", "SalonOM" or "OM Salon"); "Inner Work with Maggie"; "an Aveda Concept Salon".
- **Curly quotes and apostrophes** (’ “ ”). Use "&" only in short labels and names ("Cut & Ritual").
- **Periods, never exclamation points. No emoji.**
- **Durations** as "60 min"; prices only once confirmed.
- **Alt text:** one plain sentence saying what is in the picture. Placeholder photos say so ("Placeholder portrait of Maggie, smiling in the salon").

## Placeholders and facts

- Never publish invented testimonials, prices, credentials, addresses or hours. Until a fact is confirmed, it stays in square brackets on the page ("[Services, times, and prices to confirm.]", "[Booking link to confirm.]", "[Her training and origin story…]").
- Open facts: services, times and prices, and the Connection Call length (ask fd7da7f9); the city and address (ask bd309c65); booking links; Maggie's training and origin story (from her copy interview); Katie's page and services; real portraits; Inner Work testimonials.
- Every fact on the page comes from `shared.md` → Facts or from Maggie's copy direction. Add a new fact to `shared.md` first, with its source, then use it.
- Maggie's words are quoted word for word or not at all. Short phrases may be borrowed into our copy, but full sentences in quotation marks must match her text exactly.
